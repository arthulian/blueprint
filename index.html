A Model of
Integrity

I · Framework

Principles and Disciplines

01

Epistemic Hygiene

Credibility Appraisal

Base confidence in tried-and-true results.

Reduce confidence level when predictions fail.

Belief Updating

Revise confidence in your assumptions whenever new evidence warrants it.

Situational Analysis

Use counterfactual simulations to separate facts from situational assumptions, to identify how circumstances distort judgment.

02

Epistemic Integrity

Mirror-Testing the Strongest Case

Steelman counterarguments before reaching your own conclusions.

Mirror-test the criteria in those steelmanned arguments by rejecting those arguments that cannot survive their own criteria.

Impartial Objectivity

Prioritize factual accuracy over self-justification by deriving conclusions directly from data-driven inferences, ensuring the same empirical and rational standards are applied to every claim.

Personal Accountability

Hold your own motives and limitations to the standards of impartial objectivity and apply these standards independently of whether your conditions are favorable or unfavorable. Take full ownership of your choices and their consequences.

03

Emotional Regulation

Ego Decoupling

Tag thoughts and emotions as non-binding data points rather than components of the self, but ensure you don't invalidate core values.

Set personal boundaries to create a space (the ability to choose) between impulses and responses, allowing for rational choices.

Acceptance and Recovery

In order to enable adaptation, accept human limitations such as admitting there are periods of dependence on others.

Planned recovery should be viewed as essential for a sustained performance.

Stress Responses

Under high pressure, disengage through healthy boundaries.

Under mild/moderate pressure, use a controlled exposure to expand your stress-tolerance window.

04

Self-Concept

Self-Worth

Base intrinsic self-worth solely on your own deliberate will to align to the “four values that support integrity”.

Retain an internal locus of control by rejecting self-worth based on defensive competence or performative self-sufficiency, which are dependent on praise and criticism.

Treat setbacks as skill deficit feedback rather than personal flaws that diminish your self-worth.

Self-Confidence

Base your confidence on the verifiable achievements of your deliberate efforts.

Measure confidence in proportion to that proven competence.

Use your increased confidence to progressively tackle harder challenges.

05

Sustainable Action

Strategic Focus

Low-Power Cognition: under heavy-load, shed non-essential tasks to keep your focus on things that offer the greatest utility towards your purpose.

Avoid the illusion of always being in control, and recognize that life is often contingent on heteronomy.

Don't allow sunk costs to influence future decisions.

Sustainable Perseverance

Counter fear-driven avoidance by grounding persistence in self-kindness (self-kindness to create safety, and persistence to build momentum).

Build low-fragility through a gradual managed exposure.

Maintain commitment to long-term purpose while remaining dynamic in execution.

II · Foundation

The Four Values that support Integrity

Truthfulness

Conform beliefs, representations, and actions to objective reality as best understood.

Responsibility

Determine the proportionality between intention, knowledge, agency, and consequences, bounded by one's own limiting constraints.

Coherence

Consistently align behavior with values, beliefs with evidence, and self-concept with empirical reality.

Incorruptibility

Resist influences that compromise truth, judgment, integrity, or moral character.
